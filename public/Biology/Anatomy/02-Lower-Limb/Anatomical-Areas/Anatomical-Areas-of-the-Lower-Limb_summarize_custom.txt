This document details the lower limb's anatomy, emphasizing its critical roles in weight-bearing, locomotion, and balance. It systematically describes five main regions:

1.  **Thigh (Femoral Region):** Centered on the Femur, it houses anterior (quadriceps for knee extension), medial (adductors), and posterior (hamstrings for knee flexion/hip extension) muscle compartments, supplied by the Femoral artery and Sciatic nerve.
2.  **Knee Region:** A mobile joint formed by the Femur, Tibia, and Patella, stabilized by ligaments. The posterior popliteal fossa contains the Popliteal artery and key nerves.
3.  **Leg (Crural Region):** Comprising the Tibia and Fibula, it features four muscle compartments (anterior, lateral, deep posterior, superficial posterior) responsible for dorsiflexion and plantarflexion, supported by dedicated tibial and fibular neurovascular structures.
4.  **Ankle (Tarsal Region):** The talocrural joint connects the leg (Tibia, Fibula) with the foot (Talus and other tarsal bones). Tendons from leg muscles provide stability and enable plantar/dorsiflexion.
5.  **Foot (Pedal Region):** A complex structure of tarsals, metatarsals, and phalanges, vital for weight-bearing and shock absorption through intrinsic muscles and arches, receiving precise neurovascular supply for fine motor control and balance.
