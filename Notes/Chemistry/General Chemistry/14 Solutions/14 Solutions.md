## Overview 

In a [[Solution]], the [[Solvent]], usually the component present in greatest amounts, determines the [[State of Matter]] in which the solution exists. A [[Solute]] is a solution component dissolved in the solvent. [[Dilute Solution]] contain relatively small amounts of solute and [[Concentrated Solution]], large amounts.

Any description of the [[Composition]] of a solution must indicate the quantities of solute and solvent present. Solution [[Concentration|Concentrations]] expressed as [[Mass]] percent, [[Volume]] percent and mass/volume percent all have practical importance, as to the units, **parts per million, ppm**, **parts per billion, ppb**, and **parts per trillion, ppt**. However, the more fundamental concentration units are [[Mole]] fraction, [[Molarity]] and [[Molality]]. Molarity (moles of solute per liter of solution) is [[Temperature]] dependent, but mole fraction and molality (moles of solute per kilogram of solvent) are not.

Prediction about whether 2 [[Substance|Substances]] will mix to form a solution involve knowledge of [[Van Der Waals Forces]] between like and unlike [[Molecule|Molecules]]. This approach makes it possible to identify an [[Ideal Solution]], one whose [[Property|Properties]]can be predicted from properties of the individual solution components. Most solutions are nonideal.
