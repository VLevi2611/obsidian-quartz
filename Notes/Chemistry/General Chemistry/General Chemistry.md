## Overview

General chemistry is the study of **what everything is made of** and **how those things change**.

The world is built from tiny pieces called **atoms**.  
Atoms can connect together to form **molecules**, like LEGO pieces snapping together.

Different atoms behave differently:

- Some really want to give away electrons (metals)
- Some really want to take electrons (non-metals)
- Some don’t care at all (noble gases)

When atoms rearrange who they’re connected to, we call that a **chemical reaction**.  
Nothing disappears — atoms just switch partners. This idea is called **conservation of mass**.

To understand how reactions work, we measure:

- **Energy** (does the reaction release or need energy?)
- **Amount of stuff** (moles help us count enormous numbers of atoms)
- **Concentrations** (how crowded molecules are in a solution)

General chemistry also asks:  
**Why do reactions happen at all?**  
Because systems naturally move toward lower energy and higher disorder (entropy).

Atoms form bonds because they achieve a **more stable, lower-energy state** by sharing or transferring electrons.  
A mole is just a counting shortcut — it lets chemists talk about billions of trillions of atoms without writing impossible numbers.  
Reaction speed depends on how often and how strongly molecules collide (collision theory).  
Temperature increases particle motion → more collisions → faster reaction.  
Acids are strong when they almost completely break apart in water (ionize). Weak acids only partially ionize.
