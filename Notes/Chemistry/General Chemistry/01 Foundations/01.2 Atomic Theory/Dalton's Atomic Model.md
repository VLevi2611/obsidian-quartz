## Definition

From 1803 to 1808, John Dalton used the 2 fundamental laws of chemical combination to describe the basis of an atomic theory. His theory involved three assumptions:
1. Each chemical [[Element]] is [[Composition|Composed]] of indivisible particles called [[Atom|Atoms]]. Atoms can be neither created nor destroyed during a [[Chemical Reaction]].
2. All atoms of an element are alike in [[Mass]] and every other [[Property]]. Yet the atoms of an element are different from those of all other elements.
3. In each of their [[Compound|Compounds]], different elements combine in a simple numerical ratio: one atom A to one of B (AB), or one atom A to 2 of B (AB$_2$).
