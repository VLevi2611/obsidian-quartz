## Definition

Earlier we saw that the speed and [[Kinetic Energy]] of [[Molecule|Molecules]] vary over a wide range at any given [[Temperature]]. We also learned that molecules having kinetic energies above the average value are able to overcome intermolecular forces of attraction and escape from the surface of the [[Liquid]] into the gaseous state. This passage of molecules from the surface of a liquid into the vapor state is called **evaporation**. Evaporation occurs more readily with
- Increased temperature- more molecules have sufficient kinetic energy to overcome intermolecular forces of attraction in the liquid.
- increased [[Surface Area]] of the liquid- a greater proportion of the liquid molecules are at the surface.
- decreased strength of intermolecular forces- the kinetic energy needed to overcome the intermolecular forces of attraction is less, and more molecules have enough [[Energy]] to escape.
When [[Hydrogen Bond|Hydrogen Bonds]] are present, the [[Enthalpy]] of evaporation and the [[Boiling Point]] increase with the number of hydrogen bonds. When they are not, the enthalpy and the boiling point tend to increase with the molecular [[Polarizability]].
