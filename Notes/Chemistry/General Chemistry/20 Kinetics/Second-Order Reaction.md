## Definition

An overall **second-order reaction** has a [[Rate Equation]] with the sum of the exponents, $m, n, ... = 2$. As with [[Zero-Order Reaction|Zero]] and [[First-Order Reaction|First-Order Reactions]], this reaction will have only 1 [[Reagent]] decomposing into [[Product|Products]].
$$A \rightarrow \text{products}$$
If the [[Chemical Reaction|Reaction]] is second order, and $k$ is the [[Rate Constant]].
$$\text{rate of reaction} = k[A]^2$$
We can derive the [[Integrated Rate Law]]
$$\frac{1}{[A]_t} = kt + \frac{1}{[A]_0}$$We can see that the units of $k$ must be the reciprocal of [[Concentration]] divided by [[Time]]: $\frac{1}{M s}$
For the [[Half-Life]] of the second-order reaction, we can substitute $t = t_{\frac{1}{2}}, [A] = \frac{1}{2} [A]_0$ into the equation.
$$\frac{2}{[A]_0} = kt_{\frac{1}{2}} + \frac{1}{[A]_0}$$The half-life is $$t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{k[A]_0}$$
