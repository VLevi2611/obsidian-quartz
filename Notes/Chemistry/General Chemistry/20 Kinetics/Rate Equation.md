## Definition

One of the goals in a chemical kinetics study is to derive an equation that can be used to predict the relationship between the [[Rate of Reaction]] and the [[Concentration|Concentrations]] of [[Reagent|Reagents]], The **rate equation**. 
Consider the hypothetical [[Chemical Reaction]]
$$aA + bB + ... \rightarrow gG + hH + ...$$ Where $a,b,...$ are the [[Stoichiometric Coefficients]] in the [[Chemical Equation]]. The rate law for this reaction can be expressed in the following general form.
$$\text{rate of reaction} = k [A]^m [B]^n ...$$
The terms $[A],[B],...$ represent the reagents [[Molarity|Molarities]]. The required exponents, $m,n,...$ are generally small, positive [[Whole Number|Whole Numbers]], although in some cases they may be 0, fractional or negative. They must be determined by experiment and are generally **not** related to the stoichiometric coefficients, often $m \not = a, n \not = b, ...$
The term order is related to the exponents in the rate law and is used in 2 ways:
1. If $m = 1$, we say that the reaction is **first order in** $A$. If $n = 2$, the reaction is **second order in** $B$, and so on.
2. The overall [[Order of Reaction]] is the sum of all the exponents.
The proportionality constant $k$ relates the rate of reaction to reagent concentrations and is called the [[Rate Constant]] of the reaction.
