## Overview 

A [[Lewis Symbol]] represents the [[Valence Electron|Valence Electrons]] of an [[Atom]] by using dots placed around the chemical symbol. A [[Lewis Structure]] is a combination of Lewis symbols used to represent [[Chemical Bond|Chemical Bonds]]. Normally, all the [[Electron|Electrons]] in a Lewis structure are paired, and each atom in the structure acquires an [[Octet]], that is, there are 8 electrons as valence electrons. In [[Lewis Theory]], chemical bonds are classified as [[Ionic Bond|Ionic Bonds]], which are formed by electron transfer between atoms, or [[Covalent Bond]], which are formed by electrons shared between atoms. Most bonds, however, have partial ionic and partial covalent characteristics. 

Atoms in [[Molecule|Molecules]] are often surrounded by 8 valence electrons and thus conform to the octet rule. In covalent bonds, pairs of electrons shared between 2 atoms to form the bonds are called [[Bond Pair|Bond Pairs]], whereas pairs of electrons not shared in chemical bond are called [[Lone Pair|Lone Pairs]]. A single pair of electrons shared between 2 atoms constitutes a [[Single Covalent Bond]]. When both electrons in a covalent bond between 2 atoms are provided by only one of the atoms, the bond is called a **coordinate covalent bond**. To construct a Lewis structure for a molecule in which all atoms obey the octet rule, it is often necessary for atoms to share more than one pair of electrons, thus forming **multiple covalent bonds**. 2 shared electron pairs constitutes a [[Double Covalent Bond]] and 3 shared pairs a [[Triple Covalent Bond]].

A covalent bond in which the electron pair is not shared equally by the bonded atoms is called a [[Polar Covalent Bond]]. Whether a bond is polar or not can be predicted by comparing the [[Electronegativity]] of the atoms involved. The greater the [[Electronegativity Difference]] between 2 atoms in a chemical bond, the more polar the bond and the more ionic its character. The electron [[Charge]] distribution in a molecule can be visualized by computing an **electrostatic potential map**.

To draw the Lewis structure of a covalent molecule, one needs to know the [[Skeletal Structure]], that is, which is the [[Central Atom]] and what atoms are bonded to it. Atoms that are bonded to just one other atoms are called [[Terminal Atom|Terminal Atoms]]. Typically, the atom with the lowest electronegativity is a central atom. At times, the concept of [[Formal Charge]] is useful in selecting a skeletal structure and assessing the plausibility of a Lewis structure.

There are often exceptions to the octet rule.
