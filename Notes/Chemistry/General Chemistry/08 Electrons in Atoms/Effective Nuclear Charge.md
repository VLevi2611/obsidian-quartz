## Definition

The **effective nuclear charge**, $Z_{\text{eff}}$ , is the net positive [[Charge]] experienced by an [[Electron]] in a multielectron [[Atom]], always lower than the [[Atomic Number]] $Z$, due to the shielding effect of inner-shell electrons. It determines [[09 Periodic Trends|Periodic Trends]] such as [[Atomic Radius]], [[Ionization Energy]] and [[Electronegativity]].

$$Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - S$$ $S$ is the shielding constant (roughly the number of inner/core electrons).

$Z_{\text{eff}}$ increases across a [[Periods|Period]] from left to right because the number of [[Proton|Protons]] increases while shielding the inner electrons remains relatively constant, pulling [[Valence Electron|Valence Electrons]] closer.

$Z_{\text{eff}}$ increases only slightly down a [[Groups|Group]], as the increased shielding from additional inner shells roughly cancels out the increase nuclear charge.

Electrons with higher $Z_{\text{eff}}$ are more tightly bound to the [[Nucleus]], causing smaller atomic radii and higher ionization energies.
