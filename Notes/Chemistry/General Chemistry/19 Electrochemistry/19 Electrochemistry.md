## Overview 

In an [[Electrochemical Cell]], [[Electron|Electrons]] in an [[Oxidation Reduction]] [[Chemical Reaction|Reaction]] are transferred at [[Metal]] strips called [[Electrode|Electrodes]] and conducted through an external circuit. The [[Oxidation]] and [[Reduction]] [[Half-Cell]] reactions occur in separated regions called half-cells. In a half-cell, an electrode is immersed in a [[Solution]]. The electrodes of the 2 half-cells are joined by a wire, and an electrical connection between the solutions is also made, as through a [[Salt Bridge]]. The cell reaction involves oxidation at one electrode called the [[Anode]] and reduction at the other electrode called the [[Cathode]]. A [[Voltaic Cell]] produces [[Electricity]] from a [[Spontaneous Process|Spontaneous]] redox reaction. The [[Cell Voltage]] is the [[Potential]] difference ([[Voltage]]) between the 2 electrodes, designated as $E_{cell}$. A [[Cell Diagram]] displays the components of a cell in a symbolic way.
