## Definition

The [[Groups|Group]] 1 [[Element|Elements]], the **alkali metals**, are relatively abundant. Some of their [[Compound|Compounds]] have been known and used since prehistoric times. Yet these elements were not isolated in pure form until about 200 years ago. The compounds if the alkali metals are difficult to decompose by ordinary chemical means, so discovery of the elements had to await new scientific developments. Sodium $\ce{Na}$ and potassium $\ce{K}$ were discovered through [[Electrolysis]]. Cesium $\ce{Cs}$ and rubidium $\ce{Rb}$ were identified as new elements through their [[Atomic Spectra]]. Francium $\ce{Fr}$ was isolated in the radioactive decay products of actinium $\ce{Ac}$.
Because most alkali metal compounds are [[Water]] [[Solubility|Soluble]], a number of Lithium $\ce{Li}$, Sodium $\ce{Na}$, and potassium $\ce{K}$ compounds, including chlorides, carbonates and sulfates can be obtained from natural brines. 

By any measure, the group 1 elements are the most active [[Metal|Metals]], here are some of their [[Property|Properties]]
- **Flame colors**: The [[Energy]] differences between the $s,p$ [[Atomic Orbital|Orbitals]] of the group 1 [[Atom|Atoms]] match those of certain [[Wavelength|Wavelengths]] of visible light. As a result, when [[Heat|Heated]] in a flame, group 1 compounds produce characteristic flame colors.
- [[Density]] and [[Melting Point]]: Atoms of the group 1 elements are the largest in their respective [[Periods]], and the [[Atomic Radius|Atomic Radii]] increase from the top to the bottom within this group. These large atoms make for a relatively low [[Mass]] per unit [[Volume]], density. The lighter of the alkali metals will float on [[Water]]. The large atomic sizes, together with the fact that each of these atoms has only one [[Valence Electron]], leads to weak [[Metallic Bond|Metallic Bonding]]. They are soft metals with low melting points.
