## Definition

When used in electrochemical studies, a strip of [[Metal]], $\text{M}$ is called an **electrode**. 2 kinds of interactions are possible between metal [[Atom|Atoms]] on the electrode and metal [[Ion|Ions]] in [[Solution]]:
1. A metal ion $\ce{M^n+}$ from solution may [[Collision|Collide]] with the electrode, gain $n$ [[Electron|Electrons]] from it, and be converted to a metal atom $\text{M}$. The ion is [[Reduction|Reduced]].
2. A metal atom $M$ on the surface may lose $n$ electrons to the electrode and enter the solution as the ion $\text{M}^{n+}$. The metal atom is [[Oxidation|Oxidized]].
An [[Equilibrium]] is quickly established between the metal and the solution, which can be represented as $$\text{M}(s) \xrightleftharpoons[\text{reduction}]{\text{oxidation}} \text{M}^{n+}(aq) + ne^-$$
