## Definition

For [[Chemical Reaction|Chemical Reactions]], the **rate of reaction** describes how fast the [[Concentration]] of a [[Reagent]] or [[Product]] changes with [[Time]].

To illustrate, lets consider the reaction that begins immediately after the [[Ion|Ions]] $\ce{Fe^3+}$ and $\ce{Sn^2+}$ 
are simultaneously introduced into [[Water]]
$$\ce{2Fe^3+(aq)} + \ce{Sn^2+(aq)} \rightarrow \ce{2Fe^2+(aq)} + \ce{Sn^4+(aq)}$$Suppose that $38.5s$ after the reaction starts, the [[Molarity]], $\ce{[Fe^2+]}$ is found to be $0.0010 M$. During the period of time, $\Delta t = 38.5s$, the change in concentration of $\ce{Fe^2+}$, which we designate as $\Delta \ce{[Fe^2+]}$ is $\Delta \ce{[Fe^2+]} = 0.0010M - 0M = 0.0010M$. The average rate at which $\ce{Fe^2+}$ is formed in this interval is the change in concentration divided by the change in time.
$$\text{rate of formation of } \ce{Fe^2+} = \frac{\Delta \ce{[Fe^2+]}}{\Delta t} = \frac{0.001M}{38.5s} = 2.6 \times 10^{-5} \frac{M}{s}$$
How has the concentration of $\ce{Sn^4+}$ changed during the $38.5s$? We can see that $\Delta [\ce{Sn^4+}] = 0.00050M - 0M$. Because only [[Stoichiometric Coefficients|One]] $\ce{Sn^4+}$ ion is produced for every 2 $\ce{Fe^2+}$ ions, the buildup of $[\ce{Sn^4+}]$ will be only half that of $[\ce{Fe^2+}]$. Consequently, the rate of formation of $\ce{Sn^4+}$ is
$$\text{rate of formation of } \ce{Sn^4+} = \frac{0.00050M}{38.5s} = 1.3 \times 10^{-5} \frac{M}{s}$$
We can also follow the course of the reaction by monitoring the concentrations of the reagents. $$\text{rate of disappearance of } \ce{Fe^3+} = -\frac{\Delta \ce{[Fe^3+]}}{\Delta t} = \frac{-0.001M}{38.5s} = 2.6 \times 10^{-5} \frac{M}{s}$$
$$\text{rate of disappearance of } \ce{Sn^2+} = 1.3 \times 10^{-5} \frac{M}{s}$$The International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) recommends that we use a general rate of reaction, which for the hypothetical reaction represented by the [[Chemical Equation]], 
$$aA + bB + ... \rightarrow gG + hH + ...$$ is $$\text{rate of reaction} = -\frac{1}{a} \frac{\Delta [A]}{\Delta t} = - \frac{1}{b} \frac{\Delta [B]}{\Delta t} = ...$$
