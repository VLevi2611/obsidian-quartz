## Definition

The collective types of intermolecular [[Force|Forces]] are known as **van der Waals forces**. The intermolecular forces contributing to the term $a(\frac{n}{V})^2$ in the van der Waals equation for nonideal gases are of this type.

---
## Summary of Van Der Waals Forces

- [[London Forces]] exist between all [[Molecule|Molecules]]. They involve the [[Displacement]] of all the [[Electron|Electrons]] in molecules and they increase in strength with increasing [[Molecular Mass]]. The forces depend on molecular shapes.
- [[Dipole Dipole Interactions]] involve displacement of elector pairs in [[Chemical Bond|Bonds]] and not molecules as a whole. These forces are found only in [[Substance|Substances]] with resultant [[Dipole Moment|Dipole Moments]] ([[Polar Molecule|Polar Molecules]]). Their existence adds to the effect of London forces also present.
- When comparing substances of similar molecular size, dipole forces can produce significant differences in [[Property|Properties]].
- When comparing substances of different molecular size, London forces are usually more significant than dipole forces.
