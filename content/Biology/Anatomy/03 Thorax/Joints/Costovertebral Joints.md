## Definition

The **costovertebral **



The costovertebral joint ==connects the ribs to the thoracic spine, allowing the rib cage to expand and contract during breathing==. Located in the mid-back, these joints can experience sprains, inflammation, or osteoarthritis, causing sharp, localized pain that often worsens with deep breathing, coughing, or twisting. [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5154401/), [2](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/costovertebral-joints), [3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nas-kz18EmE), [4](https://melbourneshoulderrehab.com.au/thoracic-costovertebral-joint-sprain/), [5](https://www.physioproperth.com.au/injuries-conditions/costovertebral-joint-injury/)]

Anatomy and Function

The costovertebral articulation is complex, consisting of two main parts that connect each rib to the corresponding vertebrae: [[1](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/costovertebral-joints)]

- **Costocorporeal Joint:** The connection between the head of the rib and the sides of the vertebral bodies.

- **Costotransverse Joint:** The articulation between the tubercle of the rib and the transverse process of the vertebra. [[1](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/costovertebral-joint), [2](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/costovertebral-joints)]

Together, these synovial joints facilitate a "bucket-handle" and "pump-handle" motion, vital for respiration, while keeping the thoracic cage stable
