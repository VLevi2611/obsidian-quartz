## Overview 

The [[Joint|Joints]] of the thorax are essential for the mechanical process of **breathing**, providing the thoracic cage with the necessary flexibility to expand and retract. These articulations include both [[Synovial Joint|Synovial Joints]], which allow for free [[Movement]], and [[Solid Joint|Solid Joints]], which are linked by [[Cartilage]] and provide stability with limited mobility.

---

### Posterior Articulations (Vertebral Column)

These joints connect the ribs to the thoracic [[Vertebrae]] and facilitate the movement of the [[Rib Cage]] during respiration.

- [[Costovertebral Joints]]: Synovial joints between the **head of a rib** and the bodies of thoracic vertebrae.
    - For **ribs 2-9**, the head articulates with the superior costal facet of its own vertebra and the inferior costal facet of the vertebra above.
    - **Ribs 1, 10, 11, and 12** typically articulate with only a single corresponding thoracic vertebra.
- [[Costotransverse Joints]]: Synovial joints between the **tubercle of a rib** and the transverse process of its related vertebra. They allow for slight gliding movements and are stabilized by strong extracapsular ligaments.
- [[Thoracic Zygapophysial Joints]]: Synovial joints between the superior and inferior articular processes of adjacent vertebrae. In the thoracic region, these are **oriented vertically**, which limits flexion and extension but facilitates **rotation**.
- **[[Intervertebral Discs]]: Symphyses that connect adjacent vertebral bodies via fibrocartilage, providing cushioning and flexibility to the spine.

---

### Anterior Articulations (Sternum)

These joints connect the ribs and cartilages to the [[Sternum]], forming the anterior part of the thoracic cage.

- **[[Sternocostal Joints]]:** Articulations between the upper seven costal cartilages and the sternum.
    - The joint between **rib 1 and the [[Manubrium]]** is a **synchondrosis** (a solid, non-synovial connection made of hyaline cartilage).
    - The joints for **ribs 2-7** are typically synovial.
- **[[Manubriosternal Joint]]:** A symphysis between the manubrium and the body of the sternum. This joint forms the **[[Sternal Angle]]**, a major clinical landmark located at the **T4/T5 vertebral level**.
- **[[Xiphisternal Joint]]:** A symphysis between the body of the sternum and the xiphoid process; it often becomes ossified with age.

---

### **3. Regional and Accessory Joints**

- **[[Interchondral Joints]]:** Synovial joints that occur between the costal cartilages of adjacent ribs (primarily ribs 7 through 10). They provide indirect anchorage to the sternum and contribute to the formation of a smooth **costal margin**.
- **[[Sternoclavicular Joint]]:** A saddle-shaped synovial joint between the manubrium and the clavicle. It is the **only skeletal connection** between the upper limb (pectoral girdle) and the trunk.

---

### **Clinical and Functional Significance**

- **Respiration Mechanics:** The combination of synovial joints posteriorly and cartilaginous connections anteriorly gives the rib cage **elasticity**. During inspiration, the ribs are elevated, moving the sternum "upward and forward" (pump handle movement) and expanding the wall laterally (bucket handle movement), thereby increasing thoracic volume.
- **Sternal Angle (TIV/TV Level):** This joint is easily palpable and serves as a reference point for counting ribs (identifying rib II) and locating the bifurcation of the trachea and the beginning of the aortic arch.
- **Joint Disease:** Certain conditions like **rheumatoid arthritis** primarily affect synovial joints (like the costovertebral joints) while usually preserving symphyses (like the intervertebral discs).
