## Definition

The **pectoral girdle** is the bony ring that attaches the [[01 Upper Limb|Upper Limbs]] to the [[Skeletal System|Axial Skeleton]]. It consists of 2 [[Bone|Bones]] on each side: the [[Clavicle]] and the [[Scapula]]. It provides a critical anchor point for [[Muscle|Muscles]] and allow for a wide range of [[Movement]]. The pectoral girdle facilitates movement through 4 [[Joint|Joints]]: [[Sternoclavicular Joint]], [[Acromioclavicular Joint]], [[Shoulder Joint]] and the [[scapulothracg]]

Key Joints

The pectoral girdle facilitates movement through four primary joints: [[1](https://www.healthline.com/health/pectoral-girdle)]

- **Sternoclavicular joint:** The only direct bony connection between the pectoral girdle and the axial skeleton.

- **Acromioclavicular (AC) joint:** The connection between the scapula and the clavicle.

- **Glenohumeral joint:** The main shoulder joint, which connects the scapula to the humerus (upper arm bone).

- **Scapulothoracic joint:** The physiological joint where the scapula glides over the back of the rib cage.[[1](https://www.healthline.com/health/pectoral-girdle), [2](https://bio.libretexts.org/Courses/West_Hills_College_-_Lemoore/Human_Anatomy_Laboratory_Manual_\(Hartline\)/09%3A_Appendicular_Skeleton/9.02%3A_Shoulder_Girdle_Pectoral_Girdle)]

Function

Unlike the pelvic girdle, which is rigid and designed for weight-bearing, the pectoral girdle is highly flexible. It is only connected to the vertebral column by muscles and ligaments, which sacrifices stability for extreme mobility in the upper limbs. [[1](https://byjus.com/neet/pectoral-girdle/), [2](https://teachmeanatomy.info/pelvis/bones/pelvic-girdle/), [3](https://www.pearson.com/channels/anp/learn/bruce/the-skeletal-system/the-pelvic-girdle), [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_girdle), [5](https://study.com/academy/lesson/pectoral-girdle-bones-functions.html)]
