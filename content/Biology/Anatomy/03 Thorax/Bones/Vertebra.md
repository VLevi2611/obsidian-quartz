## Definition

Each **vertebra** is an irregular [[Bone]] that make up the [[Spinal Column]]. The proportions of the vertebrae differ according to their spinal segment. They articulate with each other to give strength and flexibility to the spinal column 




The basic configuration of a vertebra varies; the vertebral body (also _centrum_) is of bone and bears the load of the vertebral column. The upper and lower surfaces of the vertebra body give attachment to the [intervertebral discs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intervertebral_disc "Intervertebral disc"). The posterior part of a vertebra forms a **vertebral arch**, in eleven parts, consisting of two pedicles (pedicle of vertebral arch), two laminae, and seven [processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Process_\(anatomy\) "Process (anatomy)"). The laminae give attachment to the [ligamenta flava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligamenta_flava "Ligamenta flava") (ligaments of the spine). There are vertebral notches formed from the shape of the pedicles, which form the [intervertebral foramina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intervertebral_foramina "Intervertebral foramina") when the vertebrae [articulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulation_\(anatomy\) "Articulation (anatomy)"). These foramina are the entry and exit conduits for the [spinal nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve"). The body of the vertebra and the vertebral arch form the **vertebral foramen**; the larger, central opening that accommodates the [spinal canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_canal "Spinal canal"), which encloses and protects the [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").
