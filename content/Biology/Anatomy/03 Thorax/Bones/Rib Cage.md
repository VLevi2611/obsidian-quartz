The **rib cage** or **thoracic cage** is an [endoskeletal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoskeletal "Endoskeletal") enclosure in the [thorax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorax "Thorax") of most [vertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate "Vertebrate") that comprises the [ribs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rib "Rib"), [vertebral column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebral_column "Vertebral column") and [sternum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternum "Sternum"), which protect the [vital organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vital_organ "Vital organ") of the [thoracic cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_cavity "Thoracic cavity"), such as the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart"), [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung "Lung") and [great vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_vessels "Great vessels") and support the [shoulder girdle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_girdle "Shoulder girdle") to form the [core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_\(anatomy\) "Core (anatomy)") part of the [axial skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_skeleton "Axial skeleton").

A typical [human thoracic cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_skeleton "Human skeleton") consists of 12 pairs of ribs and the adjoining [costal cartilages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costal_cartilage "Costal cartilage"), the sternum (along with the [manubrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manubrium "Manubrium") and [xiphoid process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiphoid_process "Xiphoid process")), and the 12 [thoracic vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_vertebra "Thoracic vertebra") articulating with the ribs. The thoracic cage also provides attachments for extrinsic [skeletal muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") of the [neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neck "Neck"), [upper limbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_limb "Upper limb"), [upper abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_abdomen "Upper abdomen") and [back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back "Back"), and together with the overlying [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin "Skin") and associated [fascia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascia "Fascia") and [muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle "Muscle"), makes up the [thoracic wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_wall "Thoracic wall").

In [tetrapods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrapod "Tetrapod"), the rib cage intrinsically holds the [muscles of respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscles_of_respiration "Muscles of respiration")([diaphragm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm "Thoracic diaphragm"), [intercostal muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercostal_muscles "Intercostal muscles"), etc.) that are crucial for active [inhalation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhalation "Inhalation") and forced [exhalation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhalation "Exhalation"), and therefore has a major [ventilatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventilation_\(physiology\) "Ventilation (physiology)") function in the [respiratory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_system "Respiratory system").
