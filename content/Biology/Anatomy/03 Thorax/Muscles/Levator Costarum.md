## Definition

The _**levatores costarum**_ ([/ˌlɛvəˈtɔːriːz kəˈstɛərəm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")), twelve in number on either side, are small tendinous and fleshy bundles, which arise from the ends of the transverse processes of the seventh [cervical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_vertebrae "Cervical vertebrae") and upper eleven [thoracic vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_vertebrae "Thoracic vertebrae").

They pass obliquely downward and laterally, like the fibers of the [Intercostales externi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercostales_externi "Intercostales externi"), and each is inserted into the outer surface of the [rib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rib "Rib") immediately below the [vertebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebra "Vertebra") from which it takes origin, between the tubercle and the [angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_\(rib\) "Angle (rib)")(_Levatores costarum breves_).

Each of the four lower muscles divides into two fasciculi, one of which is inserted as above described; the other passes down to the second rib below its origin (_Levatores costarum longi_).

They have a role in [forceful inspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathing "Breathing").[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levatores_costarum_muscles#cite_note-1)
