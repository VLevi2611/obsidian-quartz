## Definition

The **diaphragm** is a 



The **thoracic diaphragm**, or simply the **diaphragm** ([/ˈdaɪəfræm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English");[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm#cite_note-1) [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language "Ancient Greek language"): διάφραγμα, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek "Romanization of Ancient Greek"): _diáphragma_, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation "Literal translation") 'partition'), is a sheet of internal [skeletal muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_striated_muscle "Skeletal striated muscle")[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm#cite_note-:0-2) in [humans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human "Human") and other [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal "Mammal") that extends across the bottom of the [thoracic cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_cavity "Thoracic cavity"). The diaphragm is the most important [muscle of respiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscles_of_respiration "Muscles of respiration"),[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm#cite_note-Alex-3) and separates the [thoracic cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_cavity "Thoracic cavity"), containing the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart "Heart") and [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung "Lung"), from the [abdominal cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_cavity "Abdominal cavity"): as the diaphragm contracts, the volume of the thoracic cavity increases, creating a negative pressure there, which draws air into the lungs.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm#cite_note-4)Its high oxygen consumption is indicated by the many [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondria "Mitochondria") and [capillaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillaries "Capillaries") present; more than in any other skeletal muscle.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm#cite_note-Alex-3)

The term _diaphragm_ in anatomy, created by [Gerard of Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_of_Cremona "Gerard of Cremona"),[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm#cite_note-5) can refer to other flat structures such as the [urogenital diaphragm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urogenital_diaphragm "Urogenital diaphragm") or [pelvic diaphragm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_floor "Pelvic floor"), but "the diaphragm" generally refers to the thoracic diaphragm. In humans, the diaphragm is slightly asymmetric—its right half is higher up (superior) to the left half, since the large liver rests beneath the right half of the diaphragm.
