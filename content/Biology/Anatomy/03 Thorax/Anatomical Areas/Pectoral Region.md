The **pectoral region** is ==the anterior chest area connecting the trunk to the upper limbs==. It houses the breasts and four main muscles—the **pectoralis major**, **pectoralis minor**, **subclavius**, and **serratus anterior**—which are responsible for shoulder stability, arm movement, and respiration. [[1](https://teachmeanatomy.info/upper-limb/muscles/pectoral-region/), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iZsaKvZRJpI), [3](https://www.osmosis.org/learn/Anatomy_of_the_pectoral_and_scapular_regions), [4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nhkHnwf4bjc)]

Detailed anatomy of the region includes:

- **Pectoralis Major**: The large, superficial muscle ("pecs") that flexes, adducts, and rotates the arm.

- **Pectoralis Minor**: A smaller, triangular muscle lying beneath the major muscle that stabilizes and pulls the scapula downward and forward.

- **Subclavius & Serratus Anterior**: Muscles that aid in stabilizing the shoulder joint and protracting the scapula.

- **Breast Tissue**: Located superficial to the pectoralis major muscle. [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nhkHnwf4bjc), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoralis_major), [3](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/pectoralis-major-muscle-1), [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pectoral_muscles), [5](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/pectoral-region), [6](https://www.osmosis.org/learn/Anatomy_of_the_pectoral_and_scapular_regions)]
