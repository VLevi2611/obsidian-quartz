## Definition

The **cubital fossa** is a critical **area of transition** between the [[Arm]] and the [[Forearm]], located in a triangularly shaped depression anterior to the [[Elbow Joint]]. It serves as a major gateway for neurovascular structures passing between these two regions.

### Boundaries

The fossa is defined by the following [[Muscle|Muscular]] and anatomical landmarks:

- **Lateral Border:** The [[Brachioradialis]] muscle.
- **Medial Border:** The [[Pronator Teres]] muscle.
- **Base:** An imaginary horizontal line connecting the **medial and lateral epicondyles** of the [[Humerus]].
- **Apex:** The point where the brachioradialis and pronator teres muscles meet distally.
- **Floor (Bed):** Primarily formed by the [[Brachialis]] muscle.
- **Roof:** Composed of skin and superficial fascia.

### Contents (From Lateral to Medial)

The major structures within the cubital fossa are organized in a specific order:

1. [[Biceps Brachii]] [[Tendon]]: Easily palpable and serves as a primary landmark.
2. [[Brachial Artery]]: The major [[Artery]] of the arm, which typically **bifurcates** into the radial and ulnar arteries at the apex of the fossa.
3. [[Median Nerve]]: Positioned immediately medial to the brachial artery; it leaves the fossa by passing between the two heads of the pronator teres.

### Superficial Structures and Protection

- [[Median Cubital Vein]]: Located in the roof of the fossa, it diagonally connects the **cephalic** and **basilic** veins and is a frequent site for venipuncture.
- [[Cutaneous Nerves]]: The medial and lateral cutaneous nerves of the forearm are also found within the roof.
- Bicipital Aponeurosis: A flat band of connective [[Tissue]] that passes from the biceps tendon to deep fascia, providing a protective cover for the brachial artery and median nerve.
