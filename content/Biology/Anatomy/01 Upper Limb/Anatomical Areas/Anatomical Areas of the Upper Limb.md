## Major Regions of the Upper Limb

The upper limb is divided into four main regions based on the position of its major [[Joint|Joints]] and component [[Bone|Bones]]: the shoulder, arm, forearm, and hand. Unlike the lower limb, which is designed for stability and locomotion, these regions are structurally optimized for high mobility to position the hand as a precise mechanical and sensory tool.

- **[[Shoulder]] (Pectoral Region):** The area of upper limb attachment to the trunk. It includes the **clavicle** and **scapula**, which form the pectoral girdle, and the proximal end of the **humerus**.
- **[[Arm]] (Brachial Region):** The portion of the upper limb between the shoulder and the elbow joint. Its primary skeletal support is the shaft of the humerus.
- **[[Forearm]] (Antebrachial Region):** The portion of the limb between the elbow joint and the wrist joint. It contains two parallel bones, the **radius** and the **ulna**.
- **[[Hand]] (Manual Region):** The region of the upper limb distal to the wrist joint. It is further subdivided into the **wrist (carpus)**, **metacarpus**, and **digits** (five fingers including the thumb).
