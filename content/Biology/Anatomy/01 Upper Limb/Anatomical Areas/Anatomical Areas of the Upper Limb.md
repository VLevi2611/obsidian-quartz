## Major Regions of the Upper Limb

The upper limb is a complex part of the body designed for high mobility and the precise positioning of the hand. It is divided into several major regions based on its [[Joint|Joints]] and [[Bone|Bones]]: the shoulder, arm, forearm, wrist, and hand. Unlike the lower limb, which focuses on stability and weight-bearing, the upper limb is structurally optimized to serve as a versatile mechanical and sensory tool.

- **[[Shoulder]] (Pectoral Region):** The area of upper limb attachment to the trunk. It includes the **clavicle** and **scapula**, which form the pectoral girdle, and the proximal end of the **humerus**.
- **[[Arm]] (Brachial Region):** The portion of the upper limb between the shoulder and the elbow joint. Its primary skeletal support is the shaft of the humerus.
- **[[Forearm]] (Antebrachial Region):** The portion of the limb between the elbow joint and the wrist joint. It contains two parallel bones, the **radius** and the **ulna**.
- [[Hand]]: The distal extremity of the limb, containing the palm (metacarpals) and digits (phalanges).
- [[Wrist]]: The flexible joint connecting the forearm to the hand, composed of 8 carpal bones.
