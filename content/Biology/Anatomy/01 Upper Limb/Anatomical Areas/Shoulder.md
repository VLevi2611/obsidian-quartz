## Definition

The upper limb is a complex part of the body designed for high mobility and the precise positioning of the hand. It is divided into several major regions based on its joints and bones: the shoulder, arm, forearm, wrist, and hand. Unlike the lower limb, which focuses on stability and weight-bearing, the upper limb is structurally optimized to serve as a versatile mechanical and sensory tool.

### **1. The Shoulder (Pectoral Region)**

The **shoulder** is the area where the upper limb attaches to the trunk. Its skeletal framework includes the **clavicle** and **scapula**, which form the pectoral girdle, and the proximal end of the **humerus**. It is suspended from the trunk predominantly by muscles like the **trapezius** and **deltoid**, allowing for significant movement of the scapula on the thoracic wall to extend the reach of the hand.

### **2. The Arm (Brachial Region)**

The **arm** is the portion of the limb located between the shoulder and the elbow joint. Its primary skeletal support is the shaft of the **humerus**. The arm is divided by intermuscular septa into **anterior (flexor)** and **posterior (extensor)** compartments. Muscles in the anterior compartment, such as the **biceps brachii**, primarily flex the elbow, while the posterior compartment contains the **triceps brachii**, which extends it.

### **3. The Forearm (Antebrachial Region)**

The **forearm** extends between the elbow and the wrist joints. It contains two parallel bones: the **radius** (lateral) and the **ulna** (medial). This region is unique because the radius can flip over the ulna, a movement known as **pronation**, which turns the palm posteriorly; **supination** returns the hand to the anatomical position. These movements are essential for rotating the hand without moving the shoulder or arm.

### **4. The Wrist (Carpal Region)**

The **wrist** acts as the transition zone between the forearm and the hand. It is formed by the articulation between the distal end of the **radius**, an articular disc, and a proximal row of **carpal bones**. The wrist contains the **carpal tunnel**, a critical gateway through which the median nerve and long flexor tendons pass into the palm. This joint allows the hand to be flexed, extended, abducted, and adducted relative to the forearm.

### **5. The Hand (Manual Region)**

The **hand** is the region of the limb distal to the wrist joint and is subdivided into the **carpus**, **metacarpus (palm)**, and **digits**. It serves as a sophisticated **mechanical tool** for gripping objects and a powerful **sensory tool** for touch, with a high density of receptors in the finger pads. The thumb is oriented at a right angle to the fingers, allowing for **opposition**, which is a fundamental requirement for precise manipulation.
