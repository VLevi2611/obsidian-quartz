## Definition

The [[Shoulder]] is the area of upper limb attachment to the trunk. It consists of the [[Clavicle]] and [[Scapula]], which form the pectoral girdle, and the proximal end of the [[Humerus]].

- **Key Features:** It is suspended from the trunk primarily by [[Muscle|Muscles]], which allows for significant [[Movement]] of the scapula on the thoracic wall.
- [[Joint|Joints]]: Includes the [[sternoclavicular Joint|Sternoclavicular]], [[Acromioclavicular Joint|Acromioclavicular]], and [[glenohumeral ]] (shoulder) joints.
- **Muscles:** Superficial muscles like the **trapezius** and **deltoid** provide the shoulder’s contour, while the deep **rotator cuff** muscles stabilize the joint.

### **2. The Axilla (Armpit)**

The axilla is an irregularly shaped pyramidal space that serves as the **gateway to the upper limb**. It provides the transition zone between the neck and the arm.

- **Boundaries:** Formed by the clavicle, scapula, upper thoracic wall, humerus, and related muscles.
- **Contents:** All major structures passing into the limb, including the **axillary artery**, **axillary vein**, and the **brachial plexus** nerves, pass through this space.

### **3. The Arm (Brachial Region)**

The arm is the portion of the limb between the shoulder and the elbow joint. Its primary skeletal support is the **humerus**.

- **Compartments:** The arm is divided into **anterior (flexor)** and **posterior (extensor)** compartments by medial and lateral intermuscular septa.
- **Function:** Muscles in the anterior compartment (like the biceps brachii) flex the elbow, while the posterior compartment (triceps brachii) extends it.

### **4. The Cubital Fossa**

Located anterior to the elbow joint, this triangularly shaped depression is a major **area of transition** between the arm and the forearm.

- **Contents:** From lateral to medial, it contains the **biceps brachii tendon**, the **brachial artery**, and the **median nerve**.
- **Clinical Significance:** The brachial artery in this region is often used for clinical procedures, and the superficial veins (like the median cubital vein) are common sites for blood collection.

### **5. The Forearm (Antebrachial Region)**

The forearm extends between the elbow and wrist joints. It contains two parallel bones: the **radius** (lateral) and the **ulna** (medial).

- **Compartments:** Like the arm, it is divided into anterior and posterior compartments, separated by an **interosseous membrane**.
- **Movement:** The unique relationship between the radius and ulna allows for **pronation and supination**, where the radius flips over the ulna to turn the palm posterior or anterior.

### **6. The Hand (Manual Region)**

The hand is the region distal to the wrist joint and serves as both a **mechanical tool** for gripping and a **sensory tool** for touch. It is subdivided into the **wrist (carpus)**, **metacarpus (palm)**, and **digits**.

- **Bones:** Contains 8 carpal bones, 5 metacarpals, and 14 phalanges.
- **Transition Area (Carpal Tunnel):** A gateway to the palm formed by an arch of carpal bones and the **flexor retinaculum**; it houses the median nerve and long flexor tendons.
- **The Thumb:** Positioned at a right angle to the other fingers, the thumb's specialized saddle joint allows for **opposition**, which is essential for precise manipulation of objects.
