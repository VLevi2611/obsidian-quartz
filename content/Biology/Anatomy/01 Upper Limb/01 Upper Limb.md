## Overview

The **upper limb** is a highly mobile multijointed appendage associated with the lateral aspect of the neck and the thoracic wall. Unlike the lower limb, which is optimized for stability and locomotion, the upper limb is structurally designed for **high mobility** to position the **hand** as a precise mechanical and sensory tool.

---

### [[Anatomical Areas of the Upper Limb]]

The upper limb is divided into four main regions defined by its major joints and bones. Between these regions are critical "gateways" or **areas of transition** through which neurovascular structures pass.

- [[Shoulder]]:The area of attachment to the trunk, including the **clavicle** and **scapula**.
- [[Arm]]: The portion between the shoulder and elbow, supported by the **humerus**.
- [[Forearm]]: The portion between the elbow and wrist, containing the **radius** and **ulna**.
- [[Wrist]] and [[Hand]]: The distal extremity, containing the **carpus** (8 [[Bone|Bones]]), **metacarpus** (5 bones), and **digits** (14 phalanges).
- **Key Transitions:**
    - [[Axilla]] (Armpit): The pyramidal gateway between the neck and the arm.
    - [[Cubital Fossa]]: The triangular depression anterior to the elbow.
    - [[Carpal Tunnel]]: The osteofascial gateway to the palm of the hand.

---

### [[Upper Limb Bones]]

The skeletal framework provides attachment points for muscles and acts as a lever system for [[Movement]].

- **Shoulder Girdle:** The [[Clavicle]] and [[Scapula]].
- **Arm:** The [[Humerus]].
- **Forearm:** The [[Radius]] (lateral) and [[Ulna]] (medial).
- **Hand:** Eight [[Carpals]], five [[Metacarpals]], and fourteen [[Phalanges]].

---

### [[Upper Limb Joints]]

The upper limb contains almost exclusively [[Synovial Joint|Synovial Joints]] to facilitate extensive ranges of motion.

- **Shoulder Complex:** [[Shoulder Joint]] (highly mobile ball-and-socket), [[Sternoclavicular Joint]], and [[Acromioclavicular Joint]].
- **Elbow & Forearm:** [[Elbow Joint]] (hinge for flexion/extension), [[Distal Radio-ulnar Joint]] & [[Proximal Radio-ulnar Joint]] (pivot for pronation/supination).
- **Wrist & Hand:** [[Wrist Joint]], [[Intercarpal Joints]] (saddle joint for opposition), [[Metacarpophalangeal Joints]], [[Interphalangeal Joints]], and [[Carpometacarpal Joints]].

---

### [[Upper Limb Muscles]]

[[Muscle|Muscles]] are organized into functional groups and compartments separated by deep fascia.

- **Shoulder/Back:** Muscles like the [[Trapezius]], [[Latissimus dorsi]], and the four [[Upper Limb Muscles#Rotator Cuff|Rotator Cuff]] muscles ([[Supraspinatus]], [[Infraspinatus]], [[Teres Minor]], [[Subscapularis]]).
- **Arm Compartments:** **Anterior (flexor)** compartment ([[Biceps Brachii]], [[Brachialis]], [[Coracobrachialis]]) and **Posterior (extensor)** compartment ([[Triceps Brachii]]).
- **Forearm Compartments:** **Anterior (flexor/pronator)** and **Posterior (extensor/supinator)** groups.
- [[Upper Limb Muscles#Arm|Arm]]: Small muscles entirely within the hand (thenar, hypothenar, interossei, and lumbricals) for precision tasks.

## Anatomy Terms
### **Regional and Directional Terms**

- **Axilla:** The Latin word for **armpit**.
- **Brachial / Brachium:** Refers to the **arm**, specifically the region between the shoulder and elbow.
- **Antebrachial:** Refers to the **forearm**, the region between the elbow and wrist.
- **Manual / Manus:** Refers to the **hand**.
- **Carpal / Carpus:** Refers to the **wrist**.
- **Palmar / Volar:** Refers to the **palm** (anterior surface) of the hand.
- **Dorsum:** Refers to the **back** of the hand (or the top of the foot).
- **Pollex:** The **thumb**.
- **Digital / Phalangeal:** Refers to the **fingers** (or toes).
- **Scapular:** Relating to the **shoulder blade** (scapula).
- **Clavicle:** Derived from Latin for "little key," referring to the **collarbone**.
- **Cubital:** Refers to the **elbow**; the _cubital fossa_ is the triangular depression anterior to the elbow.

### **Muscle and Bone Characteristics**

- **Biceps:** Means **two-headed** (_Bi_ = two; _Ceps_ = heads).
- **Triceps:** Means **three-headed** (_Tri_ = three).
- **Longus:** Means **long**.
- **Brevis:** Means **short** or brief.
- **Major:** Means **larger** or greater.
- **Minor:** Means **smaller** or lesser.
- **Serratus:** Means **sawtooth-like**, describing the physical appearance of the muscle attachments.
- **Deltoid:** Named for its **triangular** shape, resembling the Greek letter delta ($\Delta$).
- **Levator:** Means **lifter** or to elevate.
- **Profundus:** Means **deep**.
- **Superficialis:** Means **superficial** or closer to the surface.
- **Opponens:** Refers to the action of **opposition**, allowing the thumb to touch the other fingers.
- **Fossa:** Means a **depression** or hollow in a bone.
- **Condyle:** A rounded **articular prominence**.
- **Epicondyle:** A projection **upon or above a condyle**.

### **Functional and Anatomical Terms**

- **Pronation:** Rotation of the forearm so the **palm faces posteriorly**.
- **Supination:** Rotation of the forearm so the **palm faces anteriorly** (returning to the anatomical position).
- **Abduction:** Moving a limb or digit **away from the midline**.
- **Adduction:** Moving a limb or digit **toward the midline**.
- **Circumduction:** A **circular movement** combining flexion, extension, abduction, and adduction.
- **Anatomical Position:** The standard reference posture where the body stands erect, palms facing forward, and **thumbs pointing away** from the body.
- **Proximal:** Situated **nearer to the trunk** or point of origin.
- **Distal:** Situated **farther from the trunk** or point of origin.
