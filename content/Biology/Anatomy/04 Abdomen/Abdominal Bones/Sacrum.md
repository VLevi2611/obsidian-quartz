## Definition

The **sacrum** is a triangular [[Bone]] at the base of the [[Spinal Column]], that forms by fusi



The **sacrum** (pl.: **sacra** or **sacrums**[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrum#cite_note-1)), in [human anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_body "Human body"), is a triangular [bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone "Bone") at the base of the [spine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebral_column "Vertebral column") that forms by the fusing of the **sacral vertebrae** (S1–S5) between ages 18 and 30.[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrum#cite_note-2)

The sacrum situates at the upper, back part of the [pelvic cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_cavity "Pelvic cavity"), between the two [wings of the pelvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilium_\(bone\) "Ilium (bone)"). It forms joints with four other bones. The two projections at the sides of the sacrum are called the alae (wings), and articulate with the [ilium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilium_\(bone\) "Ilium (bone)") at the L-shaped [sacroiliac joints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacroiliac_joint "Sacroiliac joint"). The upper part of the sacrum connects with the last [lumbar vertebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumbar_vertebrae "Lumbar vertebrae") (L5), and its lower part with the [coccyx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coccyx "Coccyx") (tailbone) via the sacral and coccygeal cornua.

The sacrum has three different surfaces which are shaped to accommodate surrounding pelvic structures. Overall, it is [concave](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/concave "wikt:concave") (curved upon itself). The [base of the sacrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_of_the_sacrum "Base of the sacrum"), the broadest and uppermost part, is tilted forward as the sacral promontory internally. The central part is curved outward toward the [posterior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posterior_\(anatomy\) "Posterior (anatomy)"), allowing greater room for the [pelvic cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_cavity "Pelvic cavity").
