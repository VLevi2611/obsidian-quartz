The [external oblique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_external_oblique_muscle) is ==the largest and outermost muscle on the side of the abdomen==. It helps twist the torso, bend the spine sideways, and pull the body forward. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_external_oblique_muscle), [2](https://teachmeanatomy.info/encyclopaedia/e/external-oblique/), [3](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DGANom4IaRU/?hl=en), [4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IpD7Gc1xBoo)]

Structure and Location

- **Position:** Sits right under the skin on the side of the belly, running downward and inward like hands in front pockets.

- **Origin (Start):** Attaches to the outer surface of the lower ribs (ribs 5 through 12).

- **Insertion (End):** Connects to the hip bone (iliac crest) and the middle line of the belly (linea alba). [[1](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/ZKsoZTOrGZw), [2](https://anatomyzone.com/articles/external-abdominal-oblique-muscle/), [3](https://study.com/academy/lesson/external-oblique-definition-function-aponeurosis.html), [4](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/EloD0lzXopg), [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_external_oblique_muscle)]

Main Functions

- **Twisting:** Turns the chest to the opposite side when used on just one side.

- **Bending:** Helps tilt the body sideways.

- **Core support:** Flexes the trunk forward and squeezes the belly to help with breathing out and pushing
