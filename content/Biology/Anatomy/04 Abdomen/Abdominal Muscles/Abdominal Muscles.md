## Overview 

The [[Muscle|Muscles]] of the abdomen form a firm but [[Movement|Flexible]] wall that houses and protects the abdominal viscera, maintains intra-abdominal [[Pressure]], and assists in vital functions such as breathing and posture. These muscles are organized into two primary groups: the **anterolateral wall** and the **posterior wall**.

---

### Anterolateral Abdominal Wall

This group consists of five muscles—three flat muscles and two vertical muscles.

#### Flat Muscles

The flat muscles overlap each other laterally and transition into flat tendinous sheets called **aponeuroses** as they approach the midline.

- [[External Oblique]]: The largest and most superficial. Its fibers pass inferomedially (downward and toward the midline). It originates from the lower eight [[Ribs]] and inserts into the iliac crest and the **linea alba**. Its lower border thickens to form the **inguinal ligament**.
- [[Internal Oblique]]: The middle layer, situated deep to the external oblique. Its fibers run superomedially (upward and toward the midline), opposite to the external oblique. It originates from the thoracolumbar fascia and iliac crest, inserting into the lower ribs and linea alba.
- [[Transversus Abdominis]]: The deepest of the flat muscles. As its name suggests, its fibers run horizontally. It acts primarily to compress the abdominal contents.

#### Vertical Muscles

- [[Rectus Abdominis]]: A long, paired muscle extending the length of the anterior wall, separated by the **linea alba**. It is characterized by **tendinous intersections** that create the "six-pack" appearance. It originates from the pubis and inserts into the [[Xiphoid Process]] and costal cartilages 5–7. 
- [[Pyramidalis]]: A small, triangular muscle anterior to the rectus abdominis that may be absent in some individuals; it tenses the linea alba.

---

### Posterior Abdominal Wall

These muscles fill the framework of the posterior abdominal region and are often functionally associated with the [[02 Lower Limb|Lower Limb]].

- [[Psoas Major]]: Located medially against the [[Lumbar Vertebrae]]. It originates from the T12-L5 [[Vertebra|Vertebra]] and inserts into the lesser trochanter of the [[Femur]]. It is a powerful **hip flexor**.
- [[Iliacus]]: Fills the iliac fossa and joins the psoas major to form the **[[Iliopsoas]]** muscle as they enter the thigh.
- [[Quadratus Lumborum]]: A flat, quadrilateral muscle filling the space between rib 12 and the iliac crest. It stabilizes the twelfth rib and assists in lateral bending of the trunk.
- [[Psoas Minor]]: A slender muscle (absent in about 40% of people) that sits on the surface of the psoas major and acts as a weak flexor of the lumbar spine.

---

### Fascial Structures and Landmarks

Continuity and strength in the abdominal wall are provided by complex fascial arrangements.

- **[[Rectus Sheath]]:** A tendinous "envelope" formed by the aponeuroses of the flat muscles that encloses the rectus abdominis.
- **[[Linea Alba]]:** A midline tendinous raphe formed by the interlacing of aponeuroses from both sides, extending from the xiphoid process to the pubic symphysis.
