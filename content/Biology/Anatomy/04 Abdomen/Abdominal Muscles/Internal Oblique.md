## Definition

The **internal oblique** is a deep [[Muscle]] on the side of the abdomen that sits under the [[External Oblique]] and above the [[transve]]


The internal oblique is ==a deep, side-abdominal muscle that helps you twist your torso, bend to the side, and breathe out==. It sits underneath the external oblique muscle and above the transverse abdominis. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_internal_oblique_muscle), [2](https://brookbushinstitute.com/courses/internal-obliques), [3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dtjldAHki8o&t=4), [4](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OmeSeMZMldE), [5](https://teachmeanatomy.info/encyclopaedia/i/internal-oblique/)]

Location and Structure

- **Position:** Found on the side and front of the abdomen, tucked between outer and inner layers.

- **Fiber direction:** Runs upward and inward, perpendicular to the external oblique.

- **Origin points:** Connects to the inguinal ligament, iliac crest (hip bone), and thoracolumbar fascia (lower back).

- **Insertion points:** Connects to the lower ribs (10 through 12) and the linea alba (the center line of the belly). [[1](https://study.com/learn/lesson/internal-oblique-location-structure-function.html), [2](https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/internal-abdominal-oblique-muscle), [3](https://teachmeanatomy.info/encyclopaedia/i/internal-oblique/), [4](https://brookbushinstitute.com/courses/internal-obliques), [5](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OmeSeMZMldE)]

Key Functions

- **Rotation:** Twists the spine to the same side as the working muscle (a same-side rotator).

- **Flexion and bending:** Bends the trunk forward when both sides work together, or to the side when only one side works.

- **Pressure and breathing:** Compresses the belly organs to help push air out during heavy breathing, and supports core stability
