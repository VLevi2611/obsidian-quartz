## Definition

The **rectus abdominis** is a pair of segmented [[Muscle|Muscles]] 

The **rectus abdominis**, ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language "Latin language"): _straight abdominal_) also known as the "**abdominal muscle**" or simply better known as the "**abs**", and sometimes informally referred to as the "**six-pack**", is a pair of segmented [skeletal muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle")on the [ventral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventral "Ventral") aspect of a [person's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person "Person") [abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdomen "Abdomen"). The paired muscle is separated at the midline by a band of [dense connective tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dense_connective_tissue "Dense connective tissue") called the [linea alba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linea_alba_\(abdomen\) "Linea alba (abdomen)"), and the connective tissue defining each lateral margin of the rectus abdominus is the [linea semilunaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linea_semilunaris "Linea semilunaris"). The muscle extends from the [pubic symphysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pubic_symphysis "Pubic symphysis"), [pubic crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pubic_crest "Pubic crest")and [pubic tubercle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pubic_tubercle "Pubic tubercle") inferiorly, to the [xiphoid process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiphoid_process "Xiphoid process") and [costal cartilages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costal_cartilages "Costal cartilages") of the 5th–7th ribs superiorly.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectus_abdominis_muscle#cite_note-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectus_abdominis_muscle#cite_note-2)

The rectus abdominis muscle is contained in the [rectus sheath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectus_sheath "Rectus sheath"), which consists of the [aponeuroses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aponeurosis "Aponeurosis") of the lateral abdominal muscles. Each rectus abdominus is traversed by bands of connective tissue called the [tendinous intersections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendinous_intersections "Tendinous intersections"), which interrupt it into distinct muscle bellies.[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectus_abdominis_muscle#cite_note-3)
