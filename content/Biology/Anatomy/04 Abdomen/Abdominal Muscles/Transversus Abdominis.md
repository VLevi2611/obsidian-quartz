## Definition

The **transversus abdominis** is the deepest layer of front and side [[Muscle|Muscles]] in the abdomen. 

The transverse abdominis is ==the deepest layer of the front and side abdominal muscles==, wrapping horizontally around the torso like a natural corset to stabilize the spine and support internal organs. [[1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transverse_abdominal_muscle), [2](https://www.healthline.com/health/fitness/transverse-abdominal-exercises), [3](https://www.verywellfit.com/core-stabilization-transverse-abdominis-muscle-3120587), [4](https://www.orthocarolina.com/blog/why-the-transverse-abdominus-is-an-important-part-of-your-core)]

Location and Structure

- **Deep layer:** Sits underneath the obliques and the outer "six-pack" muscles.

- **Horizontal fibers:** Runs sideways from the ribs and spine to the front midline. [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ielt7Mj-F_I), [2](https://barmethod.com/blog/transverse-abdominis-workout-to-target-your-deepest-core-muscles/), [3](https://www.healthline.com/health/fitness/transverse-abdominal-exercises), [4](https://www.orthocarolina.com/blog/why-the-transverse-abdominus-is-an-important-part-of-your-core), [5](https://www.verywellfit.com/core-stabilization-transverse-abdominis-muscle-3120587)]

Main Functions

- **Core stability:** Protects and supports the lower back and pelvis.

- **Pressure control:** Compresses internal organs and helps with breathing, coughing, and lifting
