## Overview 

The [[Joint|Joints]] of the abdominal region primarily serve to provide a stable yet flexible framework for the posterior wall, transmit weight from the upper body to the [[02 Lower Limb|Lower Limbs]], and allow for the expansion and contraction of the [[Trunk]] during various physiological processes. These articulations include both [[Synovial Joint|Synovial Joints]] for [[Movement]] and [[Solid Joint|Solid Joints]] (symphyses) for stability.

---

### Posterior Joints (Lumbar Spine)

The [[Lumbar Vertebrae]] (L1-L5) form the central skeletal support for the posterior abdominal wall and are linked by two main types of joints.

- [[Intervertebral Discs]]: These are **symphyses** (solid joints) that connect adjacent vertebral bodies via fibrocartilage. Each disc contains a gelatinous **nucleus pulposus** that acts as a shock absorber for compression forces.
- [[Lumbar Zygapophysial Joints]]: These are **synovial joints** between the superior and inferior articular processes of adjacent [[Vertebra|Vertebrae]]. In the lumbar region, these joints are "wrapped" or oriented to facilitate flexion and extension while limiting rotation, providing stability to the lower back.

---

### Pelvic Girdle Articulations

Because the superior parts of the [[Pelvis]] form the lower boundary of the abdominal wall, their joints are critical to abdominal structure.

- [[Sacro-iliac Joints]]: Large **synovial joints** between the L-shaped articular facets of the sacrum and the ilium. They are essential for transmitting forces from the lower limbs to the vertebral column and are stabilized by strong anterior, posterior, and interosseous ligaments.
- [[Pubic Symphysis]]: A midline cartilaginous joint (symphysis) connecting the two pubic bones anteriorly. It consists of a fibrocartilaginous disc and is linked by the superior and inferior pubic ligaments. In women, this joint becomes more flexible during pregnancy to facilitate childbirth.

---

### **3. Regional and Transition Joints**

- **[[Lumbosacral Joint]]:** The articulation between the **LV vertebra** and the base of the sacrum. It marks the transition between the abdominal spine and the pelvic girdle and is stabilized by the **iliolumbar** and **lumbosacral ligaments**.
- **[[Sacrococcygeal Joint]]:** A symphysis between the apex of the sacrum and the coccyx.
- **Superior Boundary Articulations:** While often classified with the thorax, the joints between **ribs XI and XII** and the vertebrae are unique as they lack traditional anterior attachments, allowing the posterior abdominal wall to remain flexible.

---

### **Clinical Significance**

- **Back Pain:** Protrusion or herniation of the **nucleus pulposus** from an intervertebral disc can impinge on spinal nerves, causing severe pain referred to the lower limb.
- **Sacro-iliac Joint Disease:** These weight-bearing joints are susceptible to degenerative changes and inflammatory conditions such as **ankylosing spondylitis**.
- **Pelvic Fractures:** Because the pelvis forms a closed bony ring, a fracture in one location (such as near the pubic symphysis) often necessitates searching for a second fracture or joint disruption elsewhere in the ring.

### **Anatomy Dictionary: Abdominal Joints**

#### **Regional and Functional Terms**

- **Symphysis:** A type of solid joint where bones are linked by **fibrocartilage** (e.g., pubic symphysis).
- **Synovial:** A joint characterized by a **fluid-filled cavity** that allows for free movement.
- **Zygapophysial:** Derived from Greek for "yoke," referring to the **facet joints** between vertebral arches.
- **Lumbosacral:** Relating to the junction between the **lumbar** spine and the **sacrum**.

#### **Bone and Structural Terms**

- **Acetabulum:** The large **socket** on the pelvic bone that articulates with the femur.
- **Iliac Crest:** The superior margin of the **ilium**, a major landmark for the lower abdomen.
- **Pubic Tubercle:** A small bony projection on the **pubis** near the midline symphysis.
- **Intervertebral:** Literally means **"between the vertebrae"**.
- **Nucleus Pulposus:** The **gelatinous center** of an intervertebral disc.
