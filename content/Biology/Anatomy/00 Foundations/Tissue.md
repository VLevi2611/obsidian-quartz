## Definition

**Tissue** structures are groups of similar cells and their [[Extracellular Matrix]] that work together to perform a specialized function. Tissues form the structural and functional building blocks of organs, serving as the intermediate level of organization between individual cells and complete organ systems.
Tissues allow the body to compartmentalize functions, provide mechanical support, facilitate communication, and protect the internal environment.

## Organization of Tissue Types

The human body is composed of 4 primary tissue types:
-  **Epithelial Tissue** – Covers surfaces, lines cavities, and forms glands.
- **Connective Tissue** – Supports, protects, and binds other tissues together.
- **Muscle Tissue** – Produces [[Movement]] through active contraction.
- **Nervous Tissue** – Receives, processes, and transmits electrochemical signals.

## Structure and Classification of Tissues

### Epithelial Tissue

Epithelial tissues are composed of densely packed cells with minimal extracellular matrix. They are avascular (lacking [[Blood Vessels]]) and rely on diffusion from underlying connective tissue.

**Classification by Cell Layers:**
- **Simple epithelium** – A single layer of cells (optimized for absorption and filtration).
- **Stratified epithelium** – Multiple layers of cells (optimized for protection against wear and tear).
- **Pseudostratified epithelium** – Appears layered due to varying nuclear heights, but all cells touch the basement [[Membrane]].

**Classification by Cell Shape:**
- **Squamous** – Flat, scale-like cells.
- **Cuboidal** – Cube-shaped cells (equal width and height).
- **Columnar** – Tall, column-like cells.

### Connective Tissue

Connective tissue is the most abundant and widely distributed tissue type. Unlike epithelium, it consists of cells scattered throughout an extensive **Extracellular Matrix (ECM)** made of protein fibers (collagen, elastic, reticular) and ground substance.

**Major Categories:**
- **Connective Tissue Proper** – Includes loose connective tissue (e.g., adipose/fat) and dense connective tissue (e.g., tendons and ligaments).
- **Fluid Connective Tissues** – Blood and lymph.
- **Supporting Connective Tissues** – [[Cartilage]] and [[Bone]].

### Muscle Tissue

Muscle tissue is highly vascularized and consists of elongated cells ([[Muscle]] fibers) specialized for contraction using actin and myosin filaments.

**Types of Muscle Tissue:**
- **Skeletal Muscle** – Striated, voluntary, and attached to bones for movement.
- **Cardiac Muscle** – Striated, involuntary, found exclusively in the heart wall, featuring intercalated discs.
- **Smooth Muscle** – Non-striated, involuntary, found in the walls of hollow organs (e.g., Blood Vessels, stomach).

### Nervous Tissue

Nervous tissue makes up the Brain, spinal cord, and peripheral [[Nerve|Nerves]]. It is specialized for rapid communication.

**Primary Cell Types:**
- **Neurons** – Excitable cells that generate and transmit electrical impulses.
- **Neuroglia (Glial cells)** – Non-conducting cells that support, insulate, and protect neurons.

## Tissue Function

Tissues coordinate to maintain [[Homeostasis]] and execute macro-level biological processes. Main Functions:
- **Protection** – Epithelium shields internal structures from physical trauma and pathogens.
- **Support and Framework** – Connective tissues like bone and cartilage provide the body's structural scaffolding.
- **Movement** – Skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle tissues drive voluntary and involuntary locomotion.
- **Transport and Integration** – Blood transports nutrients and gases, while nervous tissue integrates systemic communication.
- **Permeability and Secretion** – Epithelial membranes control absorption (e.g., intestines) and secretion (e.g., glands).
