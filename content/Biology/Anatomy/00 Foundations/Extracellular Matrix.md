## Definition

The **Extracellular Matrix (ECM)** is a complex, non-cellular 3D macromolecular network present within all [[Tissue|Tissues]] and organs. Rather than just a passive structural scaffolding, the ECM is a dynamic environment that provides crucial biochemical and biomechanical cues that regulate cell behavior, differentiation, adhesion, and tissue [[Homeostasis]].

## Composition of the ECM

The ECM is primarily synthesized and secreted by local cells (such as fibroblasts in connective tissue) and is divided into two main classes of macromolecules:

### 1. Fibrous Structural Proteins

- **Collagen:** The most abundant [[Proteins]] in the ECM. Provides tensile strength and structural integrity. 
- **Elastin:** Provides elasticity and resilience, allowing tissues to stretch and recoil. 
- **Fibronectin and Laminin:** Cell-adhesive glycoproteins that act as "bridges," binding cells to the ECM via surface receptors.

### 2. Proteoglycans and Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)

Highly hydrated, gel-like ground [[Substance]] filling the interstitial space.    
**Function:** Because of their negative [[Charge]], they trap [[Water]], resisting compressive forces and facilitating the diffusion of nutrients and signaling [[Molecule|Molecules]].
