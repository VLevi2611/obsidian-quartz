## Definition

**Joints** are the sites where two or more skeletal elements ([[Bone|Bones]] or [[Cartilage]]) come together. Their primary function is to provide the body with structure while allowing for various degrees of [[Movement]].

## Classification of Joints

Joints are classified in two main ways: how the bones are connected (**structural**) and the amount of movement allowed (**functional**).

### Structural Classification

- **Fibrous joints:** Bones are held together by dense regular connective [[Tissue]] rich in collagen fibers.
- **Cartilaginous joints:** Bones are held together by hyaline cartilage or fibrocartilage.
- **Synovial joints:** Bones are separated by a fluid-filled cavity and joined by an articular capsule.

### Functional Classification

- **Synarthrosis:** An immovable joint (e.g., skull sutures).
- **Amphiarthrosis:** A slightly moveable joint (e.g., pubic symphysis).
- **Diarthrosis:** A freely moveable joint; all synovial joints are diarthroses.

## Solid Joints (Non-Synovial)

Solid joints lack a cavity and are held together by connective tissue or cartilage.

### Fibrous Joints

- **Sutures:** Found only in the skull; bones are linked by a thin layer of connective tissue.
- **Gomphoses:** Specialized joints between the roots of the teeth and their sockets in the jaw.
- **Syndesmoses:** Joints where bones are linked by a ligament or an interosseous membrane (e.g., between the radius and ulna).

### Cartilaginous Joints

- **Synchondroses:** Occur where hyaline cartilage connects bones, such as the growth plate in developing long bones.
- **Symphyses:** Bones are connected by a broad, flat disc of fibrocartilage (e.g., intervertebral discs and the pubic symphysis).

## Synovial Joints

Synovial joints are the most common and complex joints in the body, allowing for a wide range of motion.

### Basic Structure

- **Articular Cavity:** A narrow space between the articulating bones.
- **Articular Cartilage:** A layer of smooth **hyaline cartilage** that covers the bone surfaces to reduce friction.
- **Joint Capsule:** A double-layered sleeve enclosing the cavity, consisting of an outer **fibrous [[Membrane]]** for stability and an inner **synovial membrane**.
- **Synovial Fluid:** A viscous lubricant produced by the synovial membrane that fills the cavity.
- **Ligaments:** Strong bands of connective tissue that join bone to bone and stabilize the joint.

### Types of Synovial Joints (by Shape)

- **Plane (Gliding):** Allow sliding movements (e.g., between carpal bones).
- **Hinge:** Permit flexion and extension in one plane (e.g., elbow and knee).
- **Pivot:** Allow rotation around a longitudinal axis (e.g., atlanto-axial joint of the neck).
- **Condylar (Ellipsoid):** Allow movement in two planes (e.g., wrist joint).
- **Saddle:** Shaped like a rider in a saddle; allows extensive movement (e.g., thumb joint).
- **Ball and Socket:** The most mobile joints, allowing movement in multiple axes (e.g., shoulder and hip).
