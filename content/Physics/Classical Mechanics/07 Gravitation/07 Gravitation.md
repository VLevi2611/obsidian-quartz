## Overview

Gravitation describes the interaction between [[Mass|Masses]] through an attractive force that acts over a distance. This interaction is governed by [[Newton's Gravitation Law]], which states that every pair of masses attracts each other with a [[Force]] proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

This force gives rise to a [[Gravitational Field]], which represents how a mass influences the space around it. Objects placed in this field experience a force directed toward the source of the field.

When objects move under the influence of gravity alone, they undergo [[Central Force Motion]], in which the force always points toward a fixed center. A special case of this motion is [[Circular Orbits]], where the gravitational force provides the necessary centripetal force for motion at constant speed, determining the [[Orbital Speed]] of the object.

More generally, gravitational motion leads to a variety of orbital paths described by [[Kepler's Laws]], which relate orbital shape, period, and distance from the central body.

[[Energy]] plays a key role in gravitational systems. The motion of an object in orbit can be understood using [[Energy Of An Orbiting Body]], which includes both kinetic and gravitational potential energy. Depending on the total energy, motion can result in [[Bound vs Unbound Orbits]], where bound systems remain in orbit while unbound systems escape.

The minimum speed required for an object to escape a gravitational field is known as the [[Escape Velocity]], which depends on the mass of the central body and the distance from it.

Together, these ideas provide a complete framework for understanding planetary motion, satellite dynamics, and gravitational interactions in physical systems.
