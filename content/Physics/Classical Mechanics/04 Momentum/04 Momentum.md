## Overview

The concept of [[Linear Momentum]] provides another way to analyze motion, especially in systems involving interactions between multiple objects. Momentum is defined as the product of an object’s [[Mass]] and [[Velocity]], making it a [[Vector]] quantity that depends on both magnitude and direction.

When forces act over time, they change an object’s momentum. This effect can be described by [[Impulse]], which represents the product of force and the time interval over which it acts. The relationship between [[Force]], [[Time]], and momentum change is formalized in the [[Impulse Momentum Theorem]].

In many physical situations, it is useful to consider a [[System of Particles]] rather than a single object. Within such a system, forces can be divided into [[Internal Force]] and [[External Force]]. Internal forces act between particles inside the system, while external forces come from outside the system.

A key result is the [[Conservation Of Momentum]], which states that if the net external force on a system is zero, the total momentum remains constant. This principle is especially powerful in analyzing interactions such as a [[Collision]].

During collisions, momentum is always conserved, but kinetic energy may or may not be conserved. In an [[Elastic Collision]], both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. In an [[Inelastic Collision]], kinetic energy is not conserved, although momentum still is. A special case is a [[Perfectly Inelastic Collision]], where objects stick together after the collision.

The behavior of collisions can also be characterized using the [[Coefficient Of Restitution]], which measures how “bouncy” a collision is by comparing relative velocities before and after impact.

Another important concept is the [[Center Of Mass]], which represents the effective position of a system’s mass. The motion of the center of mass follows Newton’s laws and provides a simplified way to analyze complex systems.

Together, these ideas allow us to analyze interactions, collisions, and multi-particle systems using momentum conservation and impulse rather than directly solving force equations.
