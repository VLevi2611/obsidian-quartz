## Overview

The concept of [[Energy]] provides an alternative way to analyze motion without directly focusing on forces. Instead of tracking how forces change motion, energy methods track how different forms of energy transform and transfer within a system.

One of the most important ideas in this framework is [[Work]], which describes how a [[Force]] transfers energy to or from an object when it causes displacement. The relationship between work and motion is formalized in the [[Work Energy Theorem]], which states that the net work done on an object is equal to the change in its [[Kinetic Energy]].

[[Kinetic Energy]] is the energy associated with motion and depends on the object’s speed. As an object accelerates, its kinetic energy increases, reflecting the work done on it.

In addition to kinetic energy, systems can store energy in the form of [[Potential Energy]], which depends on position or configuration. For example, [[Gravitational Potential Energy]] arises from an object’s position in a gravitational field, while [[Elastic Potential Energy]] is stored when objects such as springs are deformed.

When only conservative forces are involved, the total mechanical energy of a system remains constant. However, in the presence of [[Non Conservative Forces]] such as friction, mechanical energy is not conserved and is instead transformed into other forms like heat.

Another important concept is [[Power]], which measures the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred over time. Power is especially useful when analyzing how quickly systems perform work.

Together, these ideas allow us to analyze physical systems using energy conservation and transformation rather than directly applying Newton’s laws.
