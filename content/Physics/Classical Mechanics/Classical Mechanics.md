## Overview

Classical Mechanics describes how objects move and interact under the influence of forces. It provides multiple complementary frameworks for analyzing physical systems, each offering a different perspective on motion.

The simplest description of motion is given by [[01 Kinematics]], which focuses on how position, velocity, and acceleration change over time without considering the causes of motion.

To understand why motion changes, we use [[02 Newton's Laws]], which relate motion to the action of [[Force]] and introduce the concept of dynamics.

In many situations, analyzing motion using forces directly can be complex. The framework of [[03 Energy]] provides an alternative approach by tracking how energy is transferred and conserved within a system.

Another powerful method is based on [[04 Momentum]], which focuses on interactions between objects. This approach is especially useful in systems involving collisions and multiple particles.

When motion involves rotation, the principles of mechanics extend into [[05 Rotation]], where linear quantities are replaced by angular analogs such as torque, angular momentum, and moment of inertia.

For systems that exhibit periodic behavior, [[06 Oscillations]] describes motion around equilibrium positions, highlighting the role of restoring forces and energy exchange.

Finally, [[07 Gravitation]] introduces long-range forces and fields, explaining orbital motion and the interaction between masses on a larger scale.

Together, these frameworks form a unified system for understanding motion, forces, energy, and interactions in the physical world.
