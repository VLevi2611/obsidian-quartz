## 📑 Concept Notes

- [[Sequences Convergence and Limits]]
    
      
    
    A real sequence converges to a limit $L$ if its terms stay within an arbitrarily small $\epsilon$-neighborhood for all sufficiently large indices. Every convergent sequence is bounded and possesses a unique limit, whereas bounded sequences may still diverge due to oscillation.
    
      
    
- **[[Limit-Arithmetic-and-Theorems]]**
    
      
    
    Limit arithmetic enables the evaluation of combined sequences through standard algebraic rules for sums, products, and quotients. Specialized results like the Squeeze Theorem and the null-times-bounded theorem provide powerful tools for trapping non-trivial sequence limits.
    
      
    
- **[[Monotonic-Sequences-and-Constants]]**
    
      
    
    The Monotone Convergence Theorem establishes that any monotonic real sequence that is bounded must converge to a finite limit. This framework provides the foundation for constructing Euler's constant $e$ and establishing asymptotic growth hierarchies.
    
      
    
- **[[Subsequences-and-Bolzano-Weierstrass]]**
    
      
    
    Subsequences are formed by selecting an infinite ordered subset of terms, automatically inheriting the limit of any convergent parent sequence. Under the Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem, every bounded sequence is guaranteed to contain at least one convergent subsequence.
    
      
    
- **[[Cauchy-Criterion-and-Partial-Limits]]**
    
      
    
    The Cauchy Criterion allows testing for sequence convergence by verifying that terms grow arbitrarily close to each other without needing the limit value in advance. Limit superior ($\limsup$) and limit inferior ($\liminf$) define the upper and lower bounds of all partial limits, equaling each other if and only if the sequence converges.
